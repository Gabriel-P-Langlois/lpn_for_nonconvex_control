# 3. PROTOCOL  (these change what the numbers MEAN)
################################################################
P1  TRAINING BOX != QUERY BOX. Train psi on [-A,A]^d with
    A = problem.train_halfwidth(a), the least A with grad psi([-A,A]) covering
    the query box. Route 1 evaluates psi at preimages that LEAVE the query box;
    Route 2 trains G at inputs y_k = grad psi(x_k) that lie strictly INSIDE it.
    QuadraticL1 A=a+t; Minplus A=((1+s)a+|mu|_inf)/s; NegL1 and ConcaveQuad
    contract, so A=a (unaffected). Improved R1 140x and R2 5.7x at d=2.
P2  SYMMETRIC EVALUATION. Same 1000 test points for both routes; unconditional
    RMSE; no abstention; NO box projection for Route 1. Designs that let Route 1
    abstain, or clamped its inverter to the training box, were RETRACTED as
    preferential treatment.
P3  ROUTE 1 = best RMSE over alpha in {0, 0.1} (Fang et al.'s regularizer).
    grad psi(y) = x - alpha*y at the solution, so the prior is biased at
    O(alpha) and the prox residual equals alpha*||y||/max(1,||x||) exactly.
    At alpha=0 the objective is coercive iff x lies in range(grad psi_theta) --
    true once psi_theta has converged (see R4). Keep the rule as cheap insurance.
    ROUTE 2 = one forward pass, no alpha: J(x) = G(x) - 0.5||x||^2.
    CERTIFICATES, same for both, no ground truth: the prox residual
    ||grad psi(y(x)) - x||/max(1,||x||), with y from the solve (R1) or
    y = grad G(x) (R2, since G ~= psi* so grad G = (grad psi)^-1); and the
    Fenchel-Young gap psi(y)+G(x)-<x,y> >= 0. Neither suffices alone: the
    residual is BLIND to divergence; FY is budget-invariant.
P4  BUDGET IS IN OPTIMIZER STEPS: S = 250000, N = 15000*d, lr 1e-3 decayed x0.1
    at 50% and 75% of S (fractions, so the schedule is invariant to S). NO early
    stopping; best-validation checkpoint kept. Epochs are derived and are not
    reported. Do NOT copy the paper's 20% decay cadence: it presumes exact
    full-batch gradients (entry 17).
P5  WIDTH FIXED AT 256, depth 2, beta 5, for every dimension -- as in the paper's
    four table captions and all 24 reported-family notebooks. The tiering was
    introduced by us on 07-08 from a PERFORMANCE observation (net oversized at
    low d), never from an experiment. Reverting also removes a confound: with
    N = 15000*d and |J| ~ 2d already growing, a jumping capacity would leave an
    error-vs-d trend attributable to nothing.
P6  Absolute RMSE is NOT comparable across d (|J| ~ 2d). Report relative error.
    N/P ranges 0.14 (d=2) to 4.6 (d=64). Runs above d=8 are opt-in
    (run_all.py --allow-high-dim).

C11 [07-16] NEW FAMILY + NEW EXPERIMENT: PosteriorMeanL1 (work2.tex
    Instantiation A), and a ONE-network design for it.

    src/targets.py    PosteriorMeanL1: the Cole-Hopf/posterior-mean smoothing of
                      J = lam*||x||_1. The given object is the PM DENOISER; the
                      prior recovered is its implicit regularizer
                      f_reg = K_eps^* - 0.5||.||^2, which has NO closed form in
                      its own argument (Louchet/Gribonval give existence only).
                      Computed to machine precision via the tangency point.
    tests/            test_posterior_mean_l1.py, 10 checks. MUST pass before any
                      number here is believed: this is a one-network experiment,
                      so a target bug would corrupt the training targets AND the
                      score they are judged against at once, with nothing
                      downstream able to catch it (cf. B1, B2, B7).
    notebooks/        posterior_mean_l1.ipynb, standalone, pre-executed.

    WHY ONE NETWORK. sections/3_main-results.tex:119 states the paper's own
    sampling model: {x_k, S(x_k,t), grad_x S(x_k,t)} -- the GRADIENT IS
    OBSERVED. In the PM regime that is not an assumption but a fact about the
    given object: the denoiser IS grad psi (one forward pass), so
    grad_x S = (x - u_PM(x))/t exactly. Lemma A.1 then makes the prior samples
    EXACT functions of the data, with no network in between:
        y_k = u_PM(x_k) = grad psi(x_k)
        f_reg(y_k)      = <x_k,y_k> - psi(x_k) - 0.5||y_k||^2   [needs S_eps]
        grad f_reg(y_k) = x_k - y_k                             [DENOISER ONLY]
    So the first network is needed ONLY when the data lacks grad S. The current
    pipeline fits psi_theta and then builds G's targets FROM psi_theta, at the
    very x_k where the exact psi(x_k) was given -- a cascade that injects
    psi_theta's error into G's targets. This experiment measures what it costs.
    Second consequence: the gradient identity needs no S_eps at all (it is prox
    optimality), and S_eps is the EXPENSIVE object in practice (a log-partition
    function; thermodynamic integration for a sampler) while the denoiser is one
    call. A gradient-only variant is therefore what transfers to a black-box
    denoiser (Instantiation B).

    ASSUMPTION IS SELF-CONSISTENT HERE, AND ONLY HERE. The theory needs
    u_PM = grad K_eps with K_eps convex. A generic trained denoiser (DnCNN,
    BM3D) is not a conservative field at all, so it is the gradient of nothing;
    that is why Fang et al. parameterize the prox as grad of an ICNN. The PM
    denoiser is exactly the class where the hypothesis is a THEOREM
    (Darbon-Langlois JMIV Prop. 3.2; Gribonval). State it that way in the paper:
    the PM denoiser is chosen BECAUSE "the prox is known" is literally true.

    CORRECTION TO work2.tex eq:upm_l1 (the .tex is NOT edited; user's call).
    It prints  u_i = x_i + t*lam*(L(a)+L(b))/(L(a)-L(b)),  which is SINGULAR at
    x_i = 0 where a = b. Differentiating eq:S_eps_l1 with L'(z) = 2z*L(z) -
    1/sqrt(pi) (the -1/sqrt(pi) cancels in the difference) gives the RECIPROCAL
    ratio, (L(a)-L(b))/(L(a)+L(b)). Then u_PM(0) = 0 by symmetry and
    u_i -> x_i -+ t*lam as x_i -> +-inf, i.e. soft thresholding as eps -> 0.
    Verified against QuadraticL1 to 1 ulp; the printed version is wrong.

    NUMERICS OF THE CLOSED FORM (all in the class docstring):
      * L(z) = 0.5*exp(z^2)*erfc(z) is NEVER formed: exp(z^2) overflows past
        z ~ 27 exactly where erfc underflows. Use log L via erfcx (z >= 0) and
        z^2 + log1p(-0.5*erfc(-z)) (z < 0).
      * psi COLLAPSES to t*eps*sum_i logaddexp(log L(a_i), log L(b_i)) -- the
        quadratic in S cancels exactly. cvx_true is OVERRIDDEN with this: the
        base class's 0.5||y||^2 - S subtracts two large near-equal numbers, and
        it is also the only form correct at t != 1 (the base identity silently
        drops the factor t).
      * The shrinkage ratio is tanh((log L(a) - log L(b))/2): algebraically
        identical, and the only stable form when one of L(a), L(b) overflows.
      * preimage: no closed-form inverse, but u_PM is separable, strictly
        increasing, and |u_PM(y)-y| < t*lam, so (x-t*lam, x+t*lam) brackets the
        root with no search. 100 bisections; inverts to 8.9e-16.

    TWO FINDINGS FROM THE TEST, both structural, neither a bug:
      * The eps -> 0 limit of the PREIMAGE is NOT uniform. Its limit
        x + t*lam*sign(x) JUMPS by 2*t*lam across the kink while every eps > 0
        preimage is continuous (u_PM(0)=0 forces preimage(0)=0), so no
        continuous family can converge to it uniformly. Off the kink the
        agreement with QuadraticL1 is 8.9e-16 (1 ulp); the error is confined to
        a layer of width ~sqrt(2*t*eps). S and f_reg DO converge uniformly --
        their limits are continuous -- so nothing we train on is affected.
      * |u_PM(y) - y| < t*lam is strict in R but ATTAINED in float64: tanh
        saturates to exactly 1.0 past |z| ~ 19. So preimage_bound is attained,
        and u_PM(A) = a exactly at A = a + t*lam. Callers must not test the
        bound strictly. This is not new: QuadraticL1 has soft(5,1) = 4 exactly.
        Coverage of the query box is ONTO WITH ZERO MARGIN by construction --
        that is what "smallest A" means -- and in exact arithmetic the excess at
        A = 5, eps = 0.1 is ~4e-37, 21 orders below the ulp at 4.

################################################################
# 6. RETRACTIONS  (recorded because the numbers moved)
################################################################
R1  "The preimage lies far outside the training box, |y|_inf ~ 19." FALSE. The
    exact preimage obeys |y*|_inf <= 5 at every d. The 19 was a diverging
    iterate whose size is set by lr*iters.
R2  "A zero prox residual certifies a preimage exists." FALSE. Where psi_theta
    is asymptotically affine, grad psi_theta(y) -> x along a DIVERGING
    minimizing sequence, so r ~ 0 is exactly what divergence looks like.
R3  "The NegL1 origin hole is a Route-2 coverage defect." FALSE. Route 1, which
    never touches G, is hit as hard (mean |err| 0.029 -> 0.342 vs Route 2's
    0.025 -> 0.276 at d=2). Shared cause: a smooth Softplus cannot represent
    psi's kink at y_i = 0, where d psi(0) = [-1,1].
R4  "Route 1 at alpha=0 is intrinsically ill-posed." FALSE. The divergence was a
    symptom of an UNDER-TRAINED psi_theta. With S=150000 no config diverges;
    quadratic_l1_8D selects alpha=0 with max|y|=5.20 against the exact bound 5
    (was 18.75). The budget-doubling measurement remains valid for the
    under-trained network it was made on.

# 8. E.2 -- THE DUALITY SANDWICH AND THE K-DECAY  [07-10]
################################################################
Code: src/maxplus_bounds.py (math + tests), bin/gamma_decay.py (study).
Outputs: logs/gamma_decay.{csv,png}, gamma_sandwich_1d.png, gamma_certificate.png
No network, no training: J = ||x||_1, t = 1, everything closed-form.

WHY NOT MaxPlus.maxplus_approx(). It builds Gamma_K and J from the SAME slope
matrix P, so growing K moves the TARGET as well as the approximant. That is a
legitimate quantity but it is not an error-decay curve. E.2 needs a fixed target
and a growing approximant. J = ||x||_1 supplies both: J* = iota_{||p||_inf<=1}
(so J*(p_k) = 0 and sampling is trivial), and S(y,1) is the separable Huber,
verified against src.targets.QuadraticL1.hjsol_true to 4e-15.

THE SANDWICH.  Gamma_K(y) = max_k {<p_k,y> - J*(p_k) - (t/2)||p_k||^2} <= S(y,t)
for EVERY K and every slope set, because each term is one competitor in the Hopf
sup. And S(.,t) is convex, so its values at samples y_m give, on conv{y_m},
S(y) <= U_M(y) := min{sum lam_m S(y_m) : lam in simplex, sum lam_m y_m = y},
one small LP per query. Neither bound touches the ground truth. Both are tight at
the samples when p_k = grad S(y_k) (the exact supporting hyperplane, E.2's
"grad g(y_k) = x_k").

THE FINDING (this is the paper-worthy part). The exponent is NOT a function of K
and d alone -- it depends on WHERE the Hopf maximizer sits, because the error
    S(y) - Gamma_K(y) = 0.5 min_k ||p_k - y||^2 - 0.5||p*(y) - y||^2
is a difference of squared distances:
  * p*(y) INTERIOR to dom J*  (||y||_inf <= 1): grad of the integrand vanishes,
    error is QUADRATIC in the fill distance -> K^{-2/d}.
  * p*(y) on the BOUNDARY (some |y_i| > 1): the constraint is active, the
    gradient does not vanish, error is FIRST ORDER -> K^{-1/d}.
On the query box [-4,4]^d the maximizer is interior on a fraction 4^{-d} of the
box, so the BOUNDARY regime governs the RMS error and a slope set that fails to
resolve d(dom J*) loses HALF the exponent. Measured (tail fit, K <= 4096):

    sampler                  d=2     d=3     d=4    predicted
    grid  (has the faces)  -1.09   -0.77   -0.63    -2/d
    uniform p in dom J*    -0.45     --    -0.32    -1/d
    uniform p, interior q  -1.01   -0.69   -0.55    -2/d   (control)

Adding only the 2^d corners does NOT repair the uniform sampler; faces of
intermediate codimension stay unresolved. Only the grid resolves every face.

SCOPE (checked against the manuscript 07-10, see CLAUDE/claude_report.tex sec 4).
This applies to the HOPF-side scheme, which approximates S by restricting
sup_p{<p,y> - J*(p) - (t/2)|p|^2}. It is VACUOUS for the paper's own PAM, which
approximates g_t = t*JBVS + q, whose conjugate psi_t is finite on ALL of R^n, so
the maximizer is never on a boundary. Relevance is prospective (E.7, general H),
plus: the paper's Thm (Gaubert) assumes Psi in C^2, which FAILS for J=||.||_1,
so this is evidence in that gap (report item L378).

RETRACTED 07-10 (was asserted here earlier the same day):
  * "tangent p = clip(y_k) attains -2/d." FALSE. Those slopes lie on the
    (d-1)-dimensional set dom(J*)'s boundary and split into ~a^-d * K interior
    points and the rest on the boundary. The two populations serve DISJOINT
    query regions and decay at different rates, so the aggregate is a MIXTURE,
    not a power law. Splitting the RMS by query location, K<=4096:
        d=2: queries in C -0.674   queries outside C -1.766
        d=4: queries in C -0.444   queries outside C -0.695
    The aggregate is dominated by the INTERIOR queries even though they are 5%
    (d=2) and 0.4% (d=4) of the box, because the boundary term collapses. The
    aggregate fit moves between -0.68 and -0.90 at d=2 across query draws.
  * "the d>=8 tangent anomaly is because dom J* has 3^d faces and K<=4096 cannot
    cover them." FALSE, or at best incidental: the mixture above holds at EVERY
    d, not only d>=8. Quote no rate for the tangent sampler.

PREASYMPTOTIC TRAPS, both live at affordable K:
  * Tensor grid: delta = 2/(m-1) but K = m^d, so small m reads too STEEP. The
    first fit gave -2.57 at d=1 (pred -2.00) purely from this. Fit the largest-K
    tail (fit_loglog_slope(..., tail=4)) -> -2.07.
  * The grid's fitted/predicted ratio drifts UP with d (1.09, 1.16, 1.26 at
    d=2,3,4) because the largest affordable m falls with d. Consistent, not
    converged.
  * Random slope sets: the fill distance of K i.i.d. points carries (log K)^{1/d},
    so a fit against log K alone understates the exponent. Entries uncorrected.

                 scored against the same y-hat used to fit, so a biased y-hat

                 stays low. Bounded here at n=2 or nowhere.

                 METHOD. At n=2, u_PM is a 2-D integral, no MCMC. Rotating to
                 s=(u1+u2)/sqrt2, r=(u1-u2)/sqrt2 puts TV on r ALONE: the
                 s-integral is then a truncated-Gaussian moment (closed form,
                 erf) and only r is quadratured, with Gauss-Legendre on each side
                 of the kink -- analytic there, so spectral. Converged to 3.3e-15
                 in n_gl; agrees with a dumb 2048^2 dense grid to 3.6e-7 (grid-
                 limited across the kink; kept as an independent witness so the
                 rotation is checked against algebra it does not share); hits the
                 w=0 truncated-Gaussian oracle to 3.3e-16.

                     sweeps  pooled err  RMS z  1-chain  shrinkage b
